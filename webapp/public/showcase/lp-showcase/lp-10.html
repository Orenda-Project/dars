Grade 5 — Creative writing

Page 15 · Activity 3

Learning Outcome (2 Minutes)

Students will be able to write a 5-sentence paragraph about their personal superpower, including at least three 3-syllable words, while demonstrating correct capitalization. [Create].

Resources & Teacher Note 💡

  • Whiteboard and colorful markers.
  • Student notebooks and pencils.
  • Visual Sketches:
    1. A simple drawing of a magnifying glass with the word "won-der-ful" broken into three parts inside it.
    2. A simple stick-figure wearing a superhero cape.

Teacher Note: A great strategy for counting syllables is to have students clap as they say the word slowly. Who remembers what a syllable is from our previous reading practice?

Opening 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Good morning, my wonderful superheroes! Today, we are going to discover our hidden talents!

Hold up a real or imaginary magnifying glass and look around the room. I am looking for students with super brain power today! Can anyone guess what my superpower is? My superpower is helping you become amazing writers! What is yours? Allow two or three students to shout out a fun superpower (like flying, super speed, or reading books really fast).

Explanation 🧠 (10 Minutes)

Draw a large circle in the center of the board and write "My Superpower" inside it. Draw branches coming out of the circle and label them "Power Words". Explain that these Power Words must have exactly three syllables (three claps).

Let me think about how I want to write my story. I need to remember to start every sentence with a big capital letter! I want my superpower to be super speed. Now, I need some big words. Let me think... 'a-maz-ing'. Let's clap it: a-maz-ing. That is three claps! I will write that on my mind map. Another word could be 'en-er-gy'. En-er-gy. Three claps! I will use these words to build my sentences.

CFU:

  • How many sentences do we need to write today? [Answer: Five sentences]
  • How many syllables should our special Power Words have? [Answer: Three syllables]
  • What must we put at the very beginning of every sentence? [Answer: A capital letter]
  • Let's clap the word 'pow-er-ful'. How many syllables does it have? [Answer: Three]

Practice ✍️ (20 Minutes)

Your minds are overflowing with stories. Let's capture them on paper!

The teacher may choose one or more of the following options based on the class's needs and available time:

Option 1: Collaborative Pair Brainstorming. Have Ali and Sara work together to clap out and list five 3-syllable words on a rough paper before they start writing their individual sentences.

Option 2: Individual Focused Writing. Students like Ahmed and Zainab will work independently in their notebooks to draft their 5-sentence paragraph. The teacher will leave the mind map on the board with a list of 3-syllable words (e.g., won-der-ful, a-maz-ing, pow-er-ful, en-er-gy, fan-tas-tic) for visual support.

Option 3: Group Word Hunt. Before writing, small groups search the chapter text on Page 15 to find 3-syllable words (like en-cour-age, ad-ven-tures) to use in their superpower paragraphs.

Differentiation:

  • Support: Provide sentence starters on the board (e.g., "My superpower is...", "I feel very...").
  • Challenge: Ask advanced students to include one 4-syllable word (like ed-u-ca-tion) in their paragraph.

When you finish, swap your notebook with your partner. Check if they started all five sentences with a capital letter!

Sample Paragraph:
My superpower is having super speed. I have endless energy to run around the city. It is amazing how fast I can help people in trouble. I feel powerful when I wear my red cape. It is a wonderful feeling to be a hero!

Conclusion 🏁 (5 Minutes)

Everyone, put your pencils down! If you used three 3-syllable words, give me a big thumbs up! If you remembered all your capital letters, pat yourself on the back!

  • What was one 3-syllable word you used, Sara?
  • Why is it important to use capital letters, Ahmed?
  • How did clapping help us today, Ali?
  • Can you share your favorite sentence with us, Zainab?

Select Zainab and Ali to stand up and read just one sentence from their paragraph out loud, emphasizing the 3-syllable word they chose, to prove they met the learning outcome.

You are all amazing writers with fantastic superpowers! Keep practicing your writing every day!
