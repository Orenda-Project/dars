Page 15 · Activity 3

Learning Outcome (2 Minutes)

Students will be able to write a 5-sentence paragraph about their imaginary superpower, including at least three 3-syllable words and correct capitalization. [Create].

Resources & Teacher Note 💡

  • Whiteboard and colorful markers
  • Students' English notebooks and pencils
  • Visual Sketches: Draw a superhero cape on the left side of the board and a magnifying glass looking at a book on the right side.

Teacher Note: A great strategy for counting syllables is to have students place their hand under their chin and count how many times their chin drops, or simply clap it out. Who can clap out the syllables in the word 'butterfly'?

Opening 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Good morning, my super students! Today, we are going to discover our hidden powers.

Let's pretend I am holding an invisible cape. If you could have any superpower to help people in Taleemabad, what would it be? Would you choose invisibility, flying, or super speed? Think about it for a moment!

Explanation 🧠 (10 Minutes)

Draw a large circle in the middle of the board and write "My Superpower" inside it. Draw branches coming out of the circle to create a Mind Map. Label the branches with "Power Words" that have exactly three syllables, such as "a-maz-ing", "pow-er-ful", and "en-er-gy".

Watch me as I plan my paragraph. I need five sentences and three words that have exactly three syllables. Let me think. 'Energy' has three claps: en-er-gy. 'Amazing' has three claps: a-maz-ing. 'Powerful' has three claps: pow-er-ful. I will start my first sentence with a capital letter. 'My amazing superpower is super speed.' See how I used a capital 'M' and my three-syllable word?

CFU:

  • Q1: How many sentences do we need to write?
  • Answer 1: Five sentences.
  • Q2: How many syllables should our three special words have?
  • Answer 2: Three syllables.
  • Q3: What must we remember to use at the beginning of every sentence?
  • Answer 3: A capital letter.

Practice ✍️ (20 Minutes)

Your minds are overflowing with stories. Let's capture them on paper!

The teacher may choose one or more of the following options based on the class's needs and available time:

Option 1: (Collaborative Pair/Group Brainstorming and Writing). Ali and Sara work together to clap out and list five 3-syllable words on rough paper before writing their own independent paragraphs.

Option 2: (Individual Focused Writing with board support). Ahmed and Zainab write independently in their notebooks, using the mind map on the whiteboard to help them spell their 3-syllable words correctly.

Option 3: (Interactive Syllable Hunt). Students write their paragraphs and then use a magnifying glass (real or drawn) to hunt for 3-syllable words in their textbook on page 15 to add to their stories.

Differentiation:

  • Support: Provide sentence starters on the board: My superpower is... It is very... I like to...
  • Challenge: Ask early finishers to include one word that has four syllables (like ed-u-ca-tion) in their paragraph.

Swap your notebooks with your partner and underline the three-syllable words they used! Check if they used capital letters!

Reference Paragraph:
My superpower is controlling water. It is an amazing ability. I can create powerful waves to clean the streets. I use my energy to give water to dry plants. Everyone is happy when I help them.
(3-syllable words used: a-maz-ing, pow-er-ful, en-er-gy)

Conclusion 🏁 (5 Minutes)

Hold up three fingers if you successfully used three three-syllable words in your story!

  • Q1: What is a syllable?
  • Q2: How many syllables are in the word 'celebration'?
  • Q3: Why do we use capital letters at the start of a sentence?
  • Q4: Can you name one three-syllable word from your story?

Select Ali and Zainab to read one sentence from their paragraphs to prove they capitalized correctly and used a 3-syllable word.

You are all wonderful writers with amazing superpowers. Keep reading and writing every day!
