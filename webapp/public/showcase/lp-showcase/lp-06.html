Objectives (2 Minutes)

Students will read multisyllabic words fluently using chunking strategies and apply appropriate expression to convey character emotions. [Application]

Resources & Teacher Support 💡

  • Textbook pages 32-33
  • Whiteboard and markers
  • A tangled piece of string or yarn

Visual Anchor: Draw a "Word Mountain" on the board to show syllable breaking. Write "res-pon-si-bil-i-ty" with each syllable climbing up and down the mountain steps to help students visualize chunking.

Teacher Note: Focus on multisyllabic chunking for longer words to maintain a steady reading pace. Who has ever had to take care of a pet or a younger sibling?

Introduction 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Bring out a tangled piece of string or yarn and try to pull it straight in front of the class, showing how difficult it is.

Good morning, class! Look at this tangled string. It has so many knots! What do you think happens if we do not brush our hair or a pet's fur for a long time?

Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (12 Minutes)

Write a long word from the story on the board. Break it into chunks using the Word Mountain strategy. Read the paragraph about Pinky forgetting her duties aloud, exaggerating the sad tone and pausing at punctuation. Emphasize the multisyllabic words clearly.

Listen carefully to how my voice changes when the story becomes sad. I am pausing at the full stops and slowing down. Be Word Detectives! When you hear the words 'responsibility', 'Whiskers', 'infection', and 'properly', circle them in your books.

  • CFU:
    • Q1: How many syllables are in the word 'infection'?
    • Answer 1: Three (in-fec-tion).
    • Q2: What does it mean when Pinky 'showed off' Whiskers?
    • Answer 2: She proudly displayed him to get attention from her friends.
    • Q3: Which silent letter is hiding in the name 'Whiskers'?
    • Answer 3: The letter 'h'.

Practice ✍️ (20 Minutes)

It is your turn to make the story come alive!

  • Option 1: Popcorn Reading. Call on students randomly to read one sentence each. Encourage them to keep the pace steady and match the emotion of the previous reader.
  • Option 2: Pair Reading. Pair Ali with Sana, and Ahmed with Sara. One student reads a paragraph while the other listens and gives feedback on their pace and expression.
  • Option 3: Individual Reading. Students read the text silently, whispering the words to themselves, focusing on chunking the longer words smoothly.

Differentiation: For students needing support, use echo reading where they repeat a sentence after the teacher, tracking words with their fingers. For a challenge, ask students to read the dialogue with distinct character voices for Pinky's parents and the vet.

Conclusion 🏁 (3 Minutes)

Ask students to hold up 1, 2, or 3 fingers to show how they found the reading today (1 = Hard, 2 = Okay, 3 = Easy).

  • Q1: Why did Pinky name her kitten Whiskers?
  • Answer 1: Because of its long whiskers.
  • Q2: What happened to Whiskers when Pinky stopped brushing his fur?
  • Answer 2: His body became covered in knots.
  • Q3: Why did Whiskers get weak and sick?
  • Answer 3: Pinky forgot to feed him and change his water.
  • Q4: What lesson did Pinky learn at the end?
  • Answer 4: Caring for someone means thinking about their needs, not just when it is fun.
