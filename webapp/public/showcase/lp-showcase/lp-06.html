1. Objectives (2 Minutes)

Students will read the text with appropriate pace and expression while accurately decoding multisyllabic words using chunking strategies.

2. Resources & Teacher Support 💡

  • Textbook (Pages 32-33)
  • Whiteboard and markers
  • A "Word Mountain" drawn on the board showing the word "responsibility" broken down into steps: re-spon-si-bil-i-ty, moving up and down the mountain.

When teaching multisyllabic words to Grade 5 students, encourage them to look for familiar chunks or syllables rather than sounding out letter by letter.

Who has a pet at home, and what do you do to take care of it?

3. Introduction 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Start the lesson by miming the action of gently petting a soft kitten in your arms, then suddenly looking at your watch, dropping your arms, and looking panicked as if you forgot something important.

Good morning, class! Let's pretend we are holding a tiny, fluffy kitten. Now, what happens if we forget to feed it or brush its fur? Today, we are going to read a story about a girl who learns a very big lesson about taking care of a pet.

4. Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (12 Minutes)

Direct the students' attention to the Word Mountain on the board. Explain that big words can be broken into smaller, easy-to-read chunks. Point to the steps of the mountain and read "re-spon-si-bil-i-ty" syllable by syllable, having the class repeat each chunk before blending the whole word together.

Read the first two paragraphs aloud to the class. Use an excited, high-pitched voice when reading about Pinky getting Whiskers and showing him off. Then, lower your voice and slow your pace to show sadness when reading about Pinky forgetting her duties.

Listen closely to how my voice changes when Pinky is happy and when she forgets her duties. While I read, you are going to be Word Detectives. Please circle or underline these words in your books: responsibility, whiskers, knots, infection, and ignore.

CFU:

  • Q1: How many syllables are in the word 'responsibility'?
  • Answer 1: Six (re-spon-si-bil-i-ty).
  • Q2: What is the silent letter hiding in the name 'Whiskers'?
  • Answer 2: The letter 'h'.
  • Q3: What does it mean when Pinky decided to 'show off' Whiskers?
  • Answer 3: She displayed him proudly to attract attention from her friends.
  • Q4: What does the text mean when it says Whiskers' body was covered in 'knots'?
  • Answer 4: It means there were tangles in his fur because he wasn't brushed.

5. Practice ✍️ (20 Minutes)

It is your turn to make the story come alive!

Option 1: Popcorn Reading. Call on a student to read a sentence or two, and then they call out "Popcorn!" and the name of another student to continue reading, keeping the class engaged and alert.

Option 2: Pair Reading. Students sit face-to-face. One reads a paragraph while the other listens, then the listener provides feedback on their partner's pace and expression before swapping roles.

Option 3: Individual Reading. Students read the text silently to themselves, focusing on pausing at commas and full stops to practice their internal pacing.

Differentiation: For students who need support, use echo reading where they repeat sentences after you, and encourage them to use finger tracking. For a challenge, ask students to read the dialogue of the parents and the vet using distinct character voices.

6. Conclusion 🏁 (3 Minutes)

Ask the students to use the Traffic Lights strategy to show how they felt about reading today's text. They should hold up a green item (or thumbs up) if it was easy, yellow (or flat hand) if it was okay, and red (or thumbs down) if they found the vocabulary difficult.

  • Q1: Why did Pinky name her kitten Whiskers?
  • Answer 1: Because the kitten had long whiskers.
  • Q2: What are two things Pinky forgot to do as the weeks passed?
  • Answer 2: She forgot to refill his water bowl, play with him, or brush his fur.
  • Q3: Why did Whiskers become weak and sick?
  • Answer 3: Because he hadn't been eating.
  • Q4: What did Pinky promise herself at the end of the story?
  • Answer 4: She promised to never ignore her responsibilities again.
