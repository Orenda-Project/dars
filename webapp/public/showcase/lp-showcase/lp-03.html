Grade 2 — Comprehension Q&A

Page 127-128 · Activity 2

1. Objectives (2 Minutes)

Students will be able to locate specific information to answer comprehension questions using keyword hunting. [Apply]

2. Resources & Teacher Support 💡

  • Textbook pages 127-128
  • Whiteboard and colorful markers
  • Visual Anchor: A drawing of a magnifying glass on the board with the words "Keyword Hunting: Find the matching words!" inside it.

Teacher Note: Keyword hunting helps students connect the question directly to the text without getting overwhelmed by every single word. When you want to find your favorite cartoon on TV fast, do you read every single channel name, or do you just look for the logo you know?

3. Introduction 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Good morning, my super detectives! Today we are going on a hunt for hidden treasures in our books!

When you lose a toy in your room, how do you look for it quickly? Do you look everywhere at once, or do you look for the color and shape of the toy?

Word of the Day: Habitat (The natural home or environment of an animal).

4. Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (10 Minutes)

The teacher writes Story 2 on the board: "The little robot beeped happily in the year 3000, in the big shiny city." The teacher then writes the question: "Where is the setting?" The teacher visually circles the word "Where" and "setting" in the question, and then draws a box around "shiny city" in the text.

I want to know where the robot is. The question asks 'Where is the setting?' I will hunt for the keyword 'setting', which means a place. I scan the sentence... I see 'shiny city'. That is a place! I found my match!

CFU:

  • Q1: What keyword tells us we are looking for a place?
  • [Answer Key: Where / Setting]
  • Q2: In Story 3, the question asks 'What is roaring?'. What keyword should we hunt for in the text?
  • [Answer Key: Roaring / Roared]
  • Q3: If a question asks 'Which animals...', what kind of word are we hunting for?
  • [Answer Key: Name of an animal / living creature]

5. Practice ✍️ (22 Minutes)

It is time to use your detective skills to conquer the textbook!

The teacher may choose one or more of the following options:

Option 1: Students work in pairs (like Ali and Fatima) to read Pinky's Story Questions on page 128. One student reads the question, and the other uses their finger to hunt for the keyword in the text to answer questions about the snow leopard.

Option 2: Students work individually. Ahmed and Zainab will silently read Activity 3 on page 128 and use their pencils to underline the keywords that prove which box is a full paragraph, then color the correct box.

Why did you choose that paragraph in Activity 3? What clues told you it was a full paragraph and not just a small piece?

Differentiation:

  • Support: Provide sentence starters on the board (e.g., "The snow leopard lives in..." or "A full paragraph has...").
  • Challenge: Ask students to write one new question about the snow leopard using a new keyword.

6. Conclusion 🏁 (3 Minutes)

AFL Strategy: Traffic Lights (Physical Response). Students put hands on their heads for Red (I am confused), hands on their shoulders for Yellow (I need a little more practice), and hands in the air for Green (I am a keyword hunting expert!).

Final Assessment Questions:

  • Q1: Where does the little robot live in Story 2?
  • Q2: What noisy animal is in the thick green jungle in Story 3?
  • Q3: According to Activity 3, what kind of spots do snow leopards have?
  • Q4: Where do snow leopards live according to the paragraph box?
