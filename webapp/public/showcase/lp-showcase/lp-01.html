Objectives (2 Minutes)

Read grade-level texts with appropriate pace, expression, and accurate decoding of two-syllable words. [Application]

Resources & Teacher Support 💡

  • Whiteboard and colorful markers
  • Textbook page 111

Visual Anchor: Draw a "Word Staircase" on the board to visually break down multisyllabic words into manageable steps (e.g., bottom step: but, middle step: ter, top step: fly).

Teacher Note: Focus heavily on the punctuation marks (exclamation points, dashes) to guide students on when to pause and when to raise their pitch for excitement or fear.

Have you ever lost sight of your parents in a crowded place like a market or a park? How did it make you feel?

Introduction 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Action: The teacher walks to the center of the room, puts a hand above their eyes as if searching far away, looks suddenly worried, mimics a fast-beating heart with their hand, and then suddenly smiles and pretends to hug someone in relief.

Good morning, my wonderful readers! Did you see me looking around just now? Imagine you are in a huge store and suddenly you cannot see your mom or dad. What would you do? Today we are going to read a diary entry about someone who had this exact adventure!

Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (12 Minutes)

The teacher introduces Phonics and 1-2 Syllable blending. Using the Word Staircase on the board, the teacher demonstrates how to break down tricky words like "flut-ter-y" and "a-ddress" by clapping out the syllables.

The teacher then models fluent reading by reading the first two paragraphs of Pinky's diary aloud. The teacher emphasizes the "thud-thud" sound of the heart, uses an excited tone for "Oh my God!", and pauses dramatically at the dashes.

As I read, I want you to be Word Detectives! Whenever you hear the words 'butterfly', 'heavy', 'weird', and 'address', draw a circle around them in your book!

CFU:

  • Q1: How many syllables are in the compound candy word 'butterfly'?
  • Answer 1: Three (but-ter-fly).
  • Q2: What does the word 'fluttery' mean when Pinky talks about her tummy?
  • Answer 2: It means feeling nervous, like having butterflies in your stomach.
  • Q3: Find a word in the first paragraph that ends with 's'. How does the 's' sound?
  • Answer 3: 'shelves' or 'words' (the 's' sounds like a 'z').
  • Q4: Spot a super easy word in the second paragraph that starts with a 'T'. What is it?
  • Answer 4: 'Then' or 'The'.

Practice ✍️ (20 Minutes)

It is your turn to make the story come alive!

Teacher's Decision Point:

  • Option 1: Popcorn Reading. The teacher calls on Ali to read a sentence, who then calls on Sara, keeping the class engaged and focused on pace.
  • Option 2: Pair Reading. Ahmed and Sana take turns reading paragraphs to each other, giving a thumbs-up when they hear good expression at the exclamation marks.
  • Option 3: Individual Reading. Students whisper-read the text to themselves, focusing on smooth pacing without stopping.

Differentiation: For support, the teacher uses echo reading and finger tracking with students struggling to decode words like 'grocery'. For a challenge, students are asked to read their assigned paragraph using Pinky's exact excited or nervous character voice.

Conclusion 🏁 (3 Minutes)

AFL Strategy: Traffic Lights. Students hold up a green pencil if they found the reading easy, a yellow pencil if it was a little tricky, or a red pencil if they need more help with the words.

Final Assessment Questions:

  1. Why did Pinky lose sight of Mama in the grocery store?
  2. What wise advice from Abbu did Pinky follow when she realized she was lost?
  3. How did Mama and Abbu react when they finally found Pinky?
  4. What two important pieces of information did Abbu say Pinky needs to learn?
