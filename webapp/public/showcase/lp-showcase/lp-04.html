Page 10 · Activity 3

Objectives (2 Minutes)

Students will be able to use the articles 'a', 'an', and 'the' correctly before naming words in simple sentences.

Teacher Support 💡

The words 'a', 'an', and 'the' are articles used before naming words (nouns). Use 'a' before words that start with a consonant sound. Use 'an' before words that start with a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u). Use 'the' when talking about a specific thing or a place that is already known (like the park, the sun).
Common Error: Students often forget the vowel rule and use 'a' before vowel sounds, writing things like "a apple" or "a umbrella".

Introduction 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Who can tell me the five vowel letters in the English alphabet?

Hold up a real apple in one hand and a book in the other hand. Show them to the students. Point to the apple and say, This is an apple. Point to the book and say, This is a book. Emphasize the sounds of 'an' and 'a' loudly.

Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (12 Minutes)

Explain that 'a', 'an', and 'the' are little helper words called articles. They sit right before naming words to make our sentences sound right. We use 'a' when the next word starts with a consonant sound. We use 'an' when the next word starts with a vowel sound. We use 'the' when we are talking about a specific place or thing that everyone knows.

Incorrect: Sana ate a orange.
Correct: Sana ate an orange.

Let's look at the word 'umbrella'. It starts with the letter 'u', which makes a vowel sound. Because it starts with a vowel sound, my brain tells me I cannot use 'a'. I must use 'an'. So, I will say 'Bilal needs an umbrella'.

Look at Activity 3 on page 10. Find the sentence about Jojo and the owl. What little word is missing?

  • Do we use 'a' or 'an' before the word 'egg'? [Expected Student Response: an]
  • What word do we use for a specific place like 'park'? [Expected Student Response: the]

Practice ✍️ (15 Minutes)

The teacher may choose one or more of the following options based on the class's needs and available time:

Option 1: Article Basket Sort. Place two baskets at the front of the room, one labeled 'A' and one labeled 'An'. Hand out picture cards of familiar items (like a mango, an onion, a pencil, an ant). Students work in pairs to decide which basket their card goes into. As they drop it in, they must say the article and the word out loud.

Option 2: Page 10 Completion. Students work individually to complete Activity 3 on page 10 in their textbooks. They will read each sentence and fill in the blanks with 'a', 'an', or 'the'.

Differentiation: For struggling learners, write the vowels (a, e, i, o, u) in large letters on the board. Have them underline the first letter of the naming word in their book before choosing their answer. For advanced learners, ask them to write two original sentences in their notebooks using 'a', 'an', and 'the' about their friends Ali and Fatima.

Conclusion 🏁 (3 Minutes)

Show a Thumbs Up if the sentence is correct, and a Thumbs Down if it is incorrect.

  • 1. We say "a apple".
  • 2. We say "an elephant".
  • 3. We say "the sun".
  • 4. We say "an book".

Final Assessment:

  1. Q1: Fill in the blank: Fatima saw ___ insect.
  2. Q2: Fill in the blank: Ali has ___ red car.
  3. Q3: Fill in the blank: We looked at ___ moon in the sky.
  4. Q4: Fill in the blank: Sana ate ___ banana.

Note: If 70% of the class fails the final assessment, re-teach the concept tomorrow using physical color-coded letter cards. Make vowels red and consonants blue. Physically match the word 'an' with red starting letters, and 'a' with blue starting letters.
