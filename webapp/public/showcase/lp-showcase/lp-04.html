Grade 2 — Grammar

Page 10 · Activity 3

Objectives (2 Minutes)

Students will be able to use the articles 'a', 'an', and 'the' correctly before naming words. They will complete sentences using the correct article.

Teacher Support 💡

Rule: Use 'a' before words starting with consonant sounds. Use 'an' before words starting with vowel sounds (a, e, i, o, u). Use 'the' when referring to a specific noun or something already mentioned.

Common Error: Students often forget to use 'an' before vowel sounds, writing things like "a apple" or "a umbrella" instead of "an apple" or "an umbrella".

Introduction 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Who can tell me what our five vowel letters are?

Hold up a real apple in one hand and a book in the other. Ask the students to point to the object that starts with a vowel sound. Then, ask them to point to the object that starts with a consonant sound.

Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (12 Minutes)

Explain to the students that in English, we use little words called articles to make our sentences sound right. These words are 'a', 'an', and 'the'. We use 'an' when the next word starts with a vowel sound (like apple or elephant). We use 'a' when the next word starts with a consonant sound (like book or mango). We use 'the' when we are talking about a specific thing that everyone knows, like the sun or a specific park.

Write an incorrect sentence on the board:

  • Incorrect: Sana ate a orange.

Ask students to find the mistake, then write the correct version:

  • Correct: Sana ate an orange.

Let's look at the word 'orange'. It starts with the letter 'o'. The sound is 'o', which is a vowel. Because it is a vowel sound, I know I must use 'an' instead of 'a'. So, I will write 'an orange'.

Direct students to open their textbooks to page 10. Look at Activity 3. Tell them to be word detectives and look at the blanks before the naming words.

CFU:

  • Q1: Do we use 'a' or 'an' before 'umbrella'? [Expected Student Response: an]
  • Q2: Do we use 'a' or 'an' before 'cat'? [Expected Student Response: a]
  • Q3: If we are talking about a specific park we all know, do we use 'a' or 'the'? [Expected Student Response: the]

Practice ✍️ (15 Minutes)

The teacher may choose one or more of the following options based on the class's needs and available time:

Option 1: Article Relay. Divide the class into two teams. Call out a word (e.g., 'egg', 'mango', 'owl'). One student from each team races to the board to tap a piece of paper that says 'a' or 'an'.

Option 2: Textbook Completion. Have students work individually to fill in the blanks for sentences 2 through 8 in Activity 3 on page 10.

Differentiation: For struggling learners, write the vowels (a, e, i, o, u) on a small card and tape it to their desks so they can check the first letter of the naming word. For advanced learners, ask them to write three original sentences in their notebooks using 'a', 'an', and 'the' about their friends Ali and Fatima.

Conclusion 🏁 (3 Minutes)

Read the following statements. Have students give a Thumbs Up if it is True, and a Thumbs Down if it is False.

  • 1. We use 'an' before the word 'ice cream'.
  • 2. We use 'a' before the word 'elephant'.
  • 3. We use 'a' before the word 'mango'.
  • 4. We use 'the' when talking about a specific place like 'the zoo'.

Final Assessment:

  1. Q1: Bilal has ___ pen. (a/an)
  2. Q2: Fatima ate ___ apple. (a/an)
  3. Q3: Look at ___ sun! (a/the)
  4. Q4: I saw ___ insect. (a/an)

Note: If 70% of the class fails the final assessment, re-teach the rule by playing a physical game. Have students stand up and make a big letter 'V' with their arms when you say a word starting with a vowel, and sit down when you say a word starting with a consonant.
