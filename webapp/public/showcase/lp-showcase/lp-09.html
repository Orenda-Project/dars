Objectives (2 Minutes)

Students will be able to identify and use reciprocal pronouns (each other, one another). They will apply this by completing sentences that demonstrate mutual actions and teamwork.

Teacher Support 💡

Grammar Rule: Reciprocal pronouns (each other, one another) are used when two or more people do the same thing to one another. They indicate a mutual action or relationship. Generally, "each other" is used for two people, and "one another" is used for more than two people.

Common Error: Students often confuse reciprocal pronouns with reflexive pronouns. For example, they might say, "Ali and Bilal looked at themselves" (meaning they looked in a mirror) instead of the correct mutual action: "Ali and Bilal looked at each other."

Introduction 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Who can tell me what it means to work as a team and share duties?

Ask two students, Sana and Ali, to stand up and give a high-five. Point out that Sana gave a high-five to Ali, and Ali gave a high-five to Sana. They did the action together.

When friends do an action together, like sharing or helping, we use special words to show that teamwork. Today, we will learn these special words!

Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (12 Minutes)

Explain that when two or more people do the exact same action to each other, we use reciprocal pronouns. Write the words "each other" and "one another" on the board.

Write an incorrect sentence on the board: Fatima and Zain helped themselves with the homework.

Wait a minute, did Fatima help her own self, and Zain help his own self? No, they were working as a team! Let's fix this.

Erase "themselves" and write the correct version: Fatima and Zain helped each other with the homework.

When I see two people doing the same action together, I use a reciprocal pronoun. If Sana helps Bilal, and Bilal helps Sana, I don't say they helped themselves. I say: Sana and Bilal helped each other. It shows teamwork! If the whole class is working together, I say they helped one another.

Direct students to page 39 in their textbooks. Ask them to look at Activity 3: Reciprocal Reception and read the incomplete sentences.

Practice ✍️ (15 Minutes)

The teacher may choose one or more of the following options based on the class's needs and available time:

Option 1: Reciprocal Freeze Tag. Using the brain break idea from page 39, students play a quick game of freeze tag. When a student is tagged and frozen, another student must come unfreeze them. Once unfrozen, the pair must high-five and say aloud a sentence using a reciprocal pronoun (e.g., "Ayesha and I helped each other").

Option 2: Sentence Completion. Students work independently in their notebooks. They must write three original sentences about their friends or family sharing duties at home, using "each other" or "one another" in every sentence.

Differentiation: For struggling learners, provide sentence frames on the board (e.g., "My brother and I help _______ clean the room."). For advanced learners, challenge them to write a short paragraph about a cricket match, using both "each other" and "one another" correctly.

Conclusion 🏁 (3 Minutes)

Read the following statements. Ask students to give a Thumbs Up if it is True, and a Thumbs Down if it is False.

  • Q1: "Each other" is a reciprocal pronoun. (Thumbs Up)
  • Q2: If Hina and Tariq smile at each other, they are doing a mutual action. (Thumbs Up)
  • Q3: "Themselves" is a reciprocal pronoun. (Thumbs Down)
  • Q4: Reciprocal pronouns show how people do things together. (Thumbs Up)

Final Assessment Questions (Oral or quick whiteboard check):

  1. Fill in the blank: The two brothers hugged _______.
  2. Fill in the blank: The five members of the team cheered for _______.
  3. Identify the error: Sara and Maha listened to themselves.
  4. Create a sentence using "each other".

Mastery Note: If more than 70% of the class struggles with the final assessment, re-teach by having two students hold opposite ends of a string. When one pulls, the other feels it, and vice versa. Use this physical visual to re-explain mutual action before trying the sentences again.
