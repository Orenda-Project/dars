Objectives (2 Minutes)

Students will be able to identify and use reciprocal pronouns ('each other' and 'one another') to show mutual actions in sentences.

Teacher Support 💡

Reciprocal pronouns (each other, one another) are used when two or more people do the same thing to one another. They express a mutual action or relationship. Traditionally, "each other" is used for two people, and "one another" is used for more than two, though they are often used interchangeably in modern English.

Common Error: Students often confuse reciprocal pronouns with reflexive pronouns. For example, they might say "Ali and Bilal helped themselves" instead of the correct "Ali and Bilal helped each other."

Introduction 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Who can tell me what teamwork means?

Call two students, Sana and Ali, to the front of the class. Ask them to give each other a high-five.

Look at Sana and Ali. Sana gave Ali a high-five, and Ali gave Sana a high-five. They high-fived each other! Today, we are going to learn about words that show teamwork like this.

Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (12 Minutes)

Explain that reciprocal pronouns are words that show people doing things together. Write the words "each other" and "one another" on the board.

Write an incorrect sentence on the board: Fatima and Hira hugged themselves.

Ask the students to find the mistake. Then write the correct version: Fatima and Hira hugged each other.

When I see 'Fatima and Hira hugged themselves,' I think, wait! Did Fatima hug only Fatima and Hira hug only Hira? No! They hugged the other person. So, they hugged each other. 'Each other' shows they did it together.

Direct students to open their textbooks to page 39 and look at Activity 3: Reciprocal Reception. Ask them to find the sentences missing their teamwork words.

Practice ✍️ (15 Minutes)

The teacher may choose one or more of the following options based on the class's needs and available time:

Option 1: Freeze Tag Duties. Play the Brain Break game from page 39. One student tags another and says a duty. When a student is unfrozen, they must look at the person who unfroze them and state a sentence using a reciprocal pronoun, such as "We help each other."

Option 2: Sentence Creation. Students write three original sentences in their notebooks using the pronouns "each other" and "one another" featuring the names of their classmates.

Differentiation:

  • Struggling Learners: Provide fill-in-the-blank sentences (e.g., "Zain and Bilal play with _____.").
  • Advanced Learners: Ask them to write a short paragraph about a cricket team winning a match, using both "each other" and "one another" correctly.

Conclusion 🏁 (3 Minutes)

Read the following statements. Ask students to give a Thumbs Up for True and a Thumbs Down for False.

  1. 'Each other' is a reciprocal pronoun.
  2. We use reciprocal pronouns when someone does a chore completely alone.
  3. 'One another' shows mutual action or teamwork.
  4. 'Themselves' is a reciprocal pronoun.

Final Assessment Questions:

  1. Ali and Bilal looked at (each other / themselves).
  2. The five friends shared their lunch with (one another / itself).
  3. Sana and Fatima respect (each other / themselves).
  4. The players congratulated (one another / him).

Mastery Note: If 70% of the class fails the final assessment, reteach the concept using a physical pairing strategy. Have students stand in pairs, face each other, mirror a physical action (like shaking hands), and repeat the phrase "we are shaking hands with each other."
