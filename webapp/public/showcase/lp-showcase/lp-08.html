Grade 5 — Comprehension Q&A

1. Objectives (2 Minutes)

Students will be able to locate specific information using keyword hunting to answer comprehension questions accurately. [Apply]

2. Resources & Teacher Support 💡

  • Textbook Page 49
  • Whiteboard and markers
  • Student notebooks and pencils

Visual Anchor: Draw a large magnifying glass on the board with three steps written inside: 1. Read Question, 2. Pick a Keyword, 3. Scan the Text.

Teacher Note: Keyword Hunting works best when students underline the most unique word in the question before they look at the passage. When you lose a toy at home, do you search the whole house randomly, or do you look in the specific places you last played with it?

3. Lesson Start 🚀 (5 Minutes)

Good morning, class! I am so excited to see my brilliant detectives today!

When you are trying to find a specific video on YouTube, what do you type in the search bar to find it quickly? (Allow students to answer). Exactly! You use specific words. Today, we will do the exact same thing with our reading to find answers fast.

Word of the Day: Cherish (to protect and care for someone or something lovingly).

4. Explanation & Teaching 🧠 (10 Minutes)

Visual Aid: The teacher writes Question 1 on the whiteboard, circles the words "Sir Riaz" in bright red, and then slowly sweeps a finger across a sample paragraph on the board, stopping dramatically when the finger lands on the matching name.

If my question asks 'What did Sir Riaz say freedom means to him?', first, I need a keyword. 'Sir Riaz' and 'freedom' are great keywords. I will scan the text just looking for the name 'Sir Riaz'. Ah, here it is! Now I will read the sentence right around his name to find my answer.

CFU:

  • Q1: What is the very first step in Keyword Hunting?
  • Answer 1: Read the question carefully and pick a keyword.
  • Q2: Should we read every single word in the text when we are just scanning for a name?
  • Answer 2: No, we just let our eyes look for the specific keyword.
  • Q3: If the question asks about Pinky, what is our best keyword?
  • Answer 3: Pinky.

5. Practice ✍️ (22 Minutes)

It is time to use your detective skills to conquer the textbook!

The teacher may choose one or more of the following options:

Option 1: Pair work. Ali and Zainab can work together. Students take turns circling the missing letters in Activity 2. Then, they underline keywords in the Activity 3 questions before scanning the text together to write the answers.

Option 2: Independent work. Ahmed and Fatima work silently, circling keywords in Activity 3, finding the answers in the text to write in their notebooks, and completing the missing letters in Activity 2 on their own.

Why is it important to pick a unique word as a keyword rather than a common word like 'the' or 'and'?

Differentiation:

  • Support: Provide sentence starters for Activity 3 (e.g., "Sir Riaz said freedom means...", "Pinky went to ask...").
  • Challenge: Ask students to write one brand new comprehension question about the text for their partner to solve using Keyword Hunting.
